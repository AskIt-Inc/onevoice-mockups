ATTR-CM trial reports 30% fewer hospitalisations at 30 months

Thirty-month follow-up from the open-label extension shows a sustained reduction in cardiovascular hospitalisation. Here is what the numbers say, and what they do not.

Written by Priya Raman

Reviewed by Dr A. Okafor, MD, Consultant cardiologist, on 29 July 2026

Source: The Lancet · Last updated 30 July 2026

On this page

    The open-label extension followed 1,142 participants for a further eighteen months after the original trial ended. The headline finding is a 30% relative reduction in cardiovascular hospitalisation compared with the placebo arm of the original study.

    What the trial measured

    The primary endpoint was the frequency of cardiovascular-related hospitalisation over the follow-up period. Secondary endpoints included six-minute walk distance and a quality-of-life score. Participants were already established on treatment, which matters when reading the result.

    “The effect held up over time, which is the question everyone asks after the first year.”

    Dr A. Okafor, reviewing this article

    What it means if you are already on treatment

    Nothing here changes day-to-day management. The finding supports staying on treatment rather than switching, and it gives your cardiologist a clearer picture when discussing long-term expectations. If you have questions about your own case, bring them to your next appointment.

    • The reduction was measured across a population, not predicted for an individual.
    • Participants were diagnosed relatively early; later-stage results may differ.
    • Open-label extensions carry a known risk of bias — the paper discusses this directly.

    What the study does not show

    It does not compare this treatment with newer agents, and it does not report on people who started treatment at a later disease stage. Both are open questions the authors flag for future work.

    Questions worth asking your team

    Whether your current treatment plan reflects this evidence, whether your follow-up interval is right, and whether any of the trial's eligibility criteria are relevant to you.
