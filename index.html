oneVoice redesign — front page for review

Visual refresh of the oneVoice patient community platform. One flexible template serving every disease indication, built to scale past 100 communities without new page designs.

⚠︎ Draft for internal review. All body copy, headlines, statistics and names are placeholder content written to test the layout — not medical information.

Internal pages — in review

  • News listing →

    The news archive: filters whose state lives in the URL, a featured lead item on page one, real pagination, and a sidebar rail. Try the filters and the community switcher.

  • News article →

    The reading page: reviewed-by credibility block, table of contents, a capped reading measure, tags linking back into the archive, and related reading.

  • Clinical trials listing →

    The trial finder — the homepage's primary call to action. Populated with real studies pulled live from ClinicalTrials.gov: genuine NCT ids, phases, sponsors and site counts, each linking to its registry record. Filters, map/list toggle, real pagination.

  • Find an expert — directory →

    Search-first, because browse-first does not work at 510 records: location and radius above the fold with real straight-line distances, facet filters that count themselves, dense rows rather than cards, and designed states for “nobody is near you” and “we could not find that place”. The people are invented, and the page says so.

  • Expert profile →

    The page that closes the credibility loop — “Reviewed by Dr A. Okafor” on an article now leads here, to the articles that reviewer signed off. No portraits anywhere: initials, which is the real production fallback. Add ?empty=1 to the URL to see the sparsest possible record, which is what most of 510 will look like.

  • Spotlight Series index →

    The archive behind the homepage band, built on the real Spotlight API: six partner programs, four published, each linking to its live microsite. This month, coming next, then the archive by year. Add ?preview=1 to see the two programs that are not published yet, and switch to another community to see the empty state — no program has been featured outside amyloidosis. The notice on the page explains which records the API is missing.

  • Understanding {indication} — patient library →

    The patient half of the education split. A numbered starting path for someone newly diagnosed, then topics with counts that derive themselves. Every record here is real — titles, publishers and topics measured out of the live content store — and where an item lives on somebody else's site the row says so and names the destination, because 64% of this library is a link, not an article. No DOI appears anywhere on the patient side: after the split there is no citation slot to fill.

  • Research and publications →

    The academic half — real papers, real journals, real DOIs, citation-first rows. The one genuinely new URL in the redesign, so it carries no redirect risk. It says “Source”, never “Journal”, because the stored field holds databases as often as journals; and there is no ISBN slot, because that field is empty across all 574 records.

  • Education detail — one template, two variants →

    Patient explainer and publication reference from one template, and hosted article vs curated hand-over from one more axis. The reviewed-by block is the trust anchor — and no reviewer field exists in the database yet, so it is shown only where it is labelled as demonstrating a proposed field, and hidden everywhere else rather than faked. Try ?a=aa-amyloidosis (hand-over), &unreviewed=1 (today's real state), ?a=the-patient-voice-development-and-results-of-a-p (a citation with no DOI, which says so instead of guessing).

Community stories and sessions remain specified and queued. Each page is designed and approved one at a time.

What to look at

One template, many communities. The colour dots switch between Amyloidosis, Sickle Cell Disease and two future indications. Only the accent colour tokens and the content change — the layout, components and markup are identical. Adding community #101 is a config file, not a redesign.

Mobile first. Every screen is designed at 390 px first: 16px+ body text, 44px+ tap targets, and a thumb-reachable bottom navigation bar.

SEO structure. Tick specs in the floating control to see the annotations — single H1 matching search intent, keyword intro above the fold, news and upcoming sessions high in the page for freshness, breadcrumbs, and Schema.org slots for NewsArticle, Event and MedicalTrial.
